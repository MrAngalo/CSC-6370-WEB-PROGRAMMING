About Me

Hello, I am Gui, and I like to troubleshoot my 3D printer. I have an Ender 3 Pro; it's about 8 years old, and it was many hobbyists' first printer because of its affordability. When I bought it, I barely used it because it was too unreliable: manual bed leveling was a nightmare, prints wouldn't stick to the bed, and it had a 50/50 chance of making it all the way to the end. For a beginner, this was a nightmare because I did not know what was wrong, and I just wanted to make cool stuff, so it sat in the garage for many years.

UNTIL NOW! I have recently been bombarded with Ender 3 videos out of the blue. Videos about upgrading it on a budget, fun challenges to do with an older 3D printer, and essential upgrades to make it faster and more reliable. Videos that I wish existed when I was first starting. Even though there are better 3D printers nowadays, I enjoy restoring and repurposing old technology. So, I got to work!

There are tons of settings: bed temperature, nozzle temperature, print speed, layer height, wall thickness, infill percentage, infill pattern, and countless many others. I had no idea what they did or which values were ideal. So I left many settings at default, except the temperatures, as they were dependent on the material that I had (which was PETG, the hardest material to print in retrospect).

I started printing benchy boats: a cute little 3d print boat that is used for benchmarking. It is extremely hard ot print, and because it is so popular, many online guides illustrate various failure points with causes and settings to tune. This was extremely helpful for my journey to make my 3D printer useful. I printed more than a dozen boats, each targeting a different problem.

When I got the boat to a somewhat satisfactory quality, it meant that I could print cool stuff! I created this website to document what I'm building and to show the problems I am facing. I would like to become the same guide that I wish I had when I was first starting. And if I can help you overcome the hurdle or even make you a little more curious about 3D printing, then it will be all worth it.
